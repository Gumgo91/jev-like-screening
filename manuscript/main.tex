\begin{abstract}
Docking the \tmALig{} ligands of DOCKSTRING against 57 pockets takes \pocketDockDaysFast{} GPU-days in the fastest search mode of Uni-Dock. A Jev-like network, named after TypeSafe's Jev, keeps most of the recall of a joint network at a fraction of its model time. It encodes the pocket once into a shared state and embeds each ligand independently of the pocket and of the other ligands, so that a cheap readout scores each pair and a screen against many pockets reduces to a matrix product. Trained on AutoDock Vina scores of \kfNTargets{} known pockets, a dual encoder and a pooled network of this organization recover \kfDualTen{} and \kfPooledTen{} of the top-1\% docking hits of new ligands in the best 10\% of the ranking, against \kfXattnTen{} for a joint cross-attention network in which every ligand atom attends to the pocket and \kfConstTen{} for a network that sees only the ligand. They keep \kfRatioDual{}\% and \kfRatioPooled{}\% of the joint recall. On one \tmAGpu{}, the dual encoder ranks the ligands against 57 pockets from SMILES in \tmADualFiftySevenFull{} s and against 1{,}000 pockets in \tmADualThousandFull{} s. The cross-attention network needs \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} times the model time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times for 1{,}000 pockets. From SMILES to scores the ratios are \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenFull{} and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandFull{}, because the featurization of the ligands, which every network needs, dominates the time of the dual encoder. The networks serve only the pockets of their training data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusions}

Jev-like networks encode a pocket once into a shared state and screen a library against hundreds of pockets in a small fraction of the model time of a joint network. On \kfNTargets{} known pockets the dual encoder keeps \kfRatioDual{}\% and the pooled network \kfRatioPooled{}\% of the recall of a cross-attention network at a budget of the best 10\% of the ranking. The cross-attention network needs \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} times the model time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times for 1{,}000 pockets. From SMILES to scores it needs \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenFull{} times the time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandFull{} times for 1{,}000 pockets. The dual encoder ranks \tmALig{} ligands against 57 pockets from SMILES in \tmADualFiftySevenFull{} s on one \tmAGpu{}, whereas the docking of the same pairs takes \pocketDockDaysFast{} GPU-days in the fastest search mode of Uni-Dock.

\section{Data and Software Availability}

The GitHub repository \url{https://github.com/Gumgo91/jev-like-screening} holds the DockMut-1 re-docking scores, the receptor variant manifests, the seed-1 wild-type poses, the evaluation outputs, the registered analysis plan with its hash ledger and all code. The trained checkpoints, the baseline checkpoints and the cached pocket graphs exceed the size limits of a git repository. They are in the archive \texttt{DockMut\_release.zip} of release v1.0 of the repository. The archive holds the complete file tree and the SHA-256 hash of every file. The receptors and scores of the DOCKSTRING dataset are available from the DOCKSTRING repository and figshare under the Apache License 2.0. Docking ran in a micromamba environment with Python 3.11, Uni-Dock 1.2.0\cite{unidock} and Open Babel\cite{openbabel} (\texttt{code/pod\_setup.sh} of the archive). The dual encoders, the constant-pocket networks and the residue-sum networks of the preceding analysis were trained on RunPod GPU pods. The pooled and cross-attention networks of the existing group were trained on a desktop with one RTX 4060. The cross-fitted networks, their evaluation and the full-scale timing (RTX 4090, with an L40S as second measurement) ran on RunPod pods. The docking rate and the network throughput were timed on a RunPod pod with one RTX 4090 (PyTorch 2.4.1, Open Babel 3.2.1). The records of every call are in the archive. The remaining evaluation and analysis ran on a Windows workstation with Python 3.13.5, PyTorch 2.7.1,\cite{paszke2019} scikit-learn 1.7.1\cite{sklearn} and RDKit 2026.03.6\cite{rdkit}.
