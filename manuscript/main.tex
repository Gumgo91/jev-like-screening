\begin{abstract}
Docking a large library against many pockets is limited by the cost of the calculation. Jev-like networks encode each pocket once into a shared state and score ligands against it independently, which keeps the cost of a screen against many pockets low. Trained on the AutoDock Vina scores of known pockets of DOCKSTRING, a dual encoder and a pooled network of this organization recover \kfDualTen{} and \kfPooledTen{} of the top-1\% docking hits of new ligands for these pockets in the best 10\% of the ranking, against \kfXattnTen{} for a cross-attention network in which every ligand atom attends to the pocket and \kfConstTen{} for a network that sees only the ligand. Measured at the full scale on one \tmAGpu{}, the dual encoder ranks the \tmALig{} ligands of the library against 57 pockets from SMILES in \tmADualFiftySevenFull{} s and against 1{,}000 pockets in \tmADualThousandFull{} s, whereas the fastest search mode of Uni-Dock needs \pocketDockDaysFast{} GPU-days for the 57 pockets. The cross-attention network needs \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} times the model time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times for 1{,}000 pockets, which is \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenFull{} and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandFull{} times from SMILES to scores, because the featurization of the ligands, which every network needs, dominates the time of the dual encoder. The networks serve the pockets of their training data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusions}

Jev-like networks that encode a pocket once into a shared state screen a library against hundreds of pockets at a small part of the model time of a joint network. On \kfNTargets{} known pockets the dual encoder and the pooled network keep \kfRatioDual{}\% and \kfRatioPooled{}\% of the recall of a cross-attention network at a budget of the best 10\% of the ranking, and the cross-attention network needs \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} times their model time for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times for 1{,}000 pockets (\tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenFull{} and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandFull{} times from SMILES to scores). The dual encoder ranks \tmALig{} ligands against 57 pockets from SMILES in \tmADualFiftySevenFull{} s on one \tmAGpu{}, against \pocketDockDaysFast{} GPU-days for the docking of the same pairs in the fastest search mode of Uni-Dock.

\section{Data and Software Availability}

The DockMut-1 re-docking scores, receptor variant manifests, seed-1 wild-type poses, evaluation outputs, the registered analysis plan with its hash ledger and all code are deposited in the GitHub repository \url{https://github.com/Gumgo91/jev-like-screening}. The trained checkpoints, the baseline checkpoints and the cached pocket graphs exceed the size limits of a git repository and are in the archive \texttt{DockMut\_release.zip} of release v1.0 of the repository, which holds the complete file tree and the SHA-256 hash of every file. The receptors and scores of the DOCKSTRING dataset are available from the DOCKSTRING repository and figshare under the Apache License 2.0. Docking ran in a micromamba environment with Python 3.11, Uni-Dock 1.2.0 and Open Babel (\texttt{code/pod\_setup.sh} of the archive), and the dual encoders, the constant-pocket networks and the residue-sum networks of the preceding analysis were trained on RunPod GPU pods, the pooled and cross-attention networks of the preceding analysis on a desktop with one RTX 4060, and the cross-fitted networks, their evaluation and the full-scale timing (RTX 4090, with an L40S as second measurement) ran on RunPod pods. The docking rate and the network throughput were timed on a RunPod pod with one RTX 4090 (PyTorch 2.4.1, Open Babel 3.2.1), and the records of every call are in the archive. Evaluation and analysis ran on a Windows workstation with Python 3.13.5, PyTorch 2.7.1, scikit-learn 1.7.1 and RDKit 2026.03.6.
