\subsection{A cached pocket state screens a library against many pockets in seconds}

A Jev-like network embeds every ligand once and pays one pocket state per pocket, whereas a joint network pays its interaction layers for every pair. Table~\ref{tab:cost} and Figure~\ref{fig:cost}a,b give the cost of screening all \tmALig{} ligands of DOCKSTRING from SMILES on one \tmAGpu{}, measured at the full scale. The dual encoder needs \tmADualOneModel{} s to embed the library, and the scoring of a pocket is a matrix product, so that the model time grows from \tmADualOneModel{} s for one pocket to \tmADualFiftySevenModel{} s for 57 pockets and \tmADualThousandModel{} s for 1{,}000 pockets. The scoring of the \pocketPairs{} million pairs of 57 pockets and the selection of the best 10\% per pocket take \tmADualFiftySevenSelect{} s, a rate of \tmADualFiftySevenRate{} million pairs per second. The pooled network costs the same as the dual encoder for 57 pockets (\tmAPooledFiftySevenModel{} s) and \tmARatioPooledThousandModel{} times as much for 1{,}000 pockets (\tmAPooledThousandModel{} s), because its first layer is decomposed into a ligand term and a pocket term that are computed once.

The cross-attention network scores \tmAXaHalfFiftySevenRate{} million pairs per second in float16 and \tmAXaSingleFiftySevenRate{} in float32, which gives a model time of \tmAXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} and \tmAXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} s for 57 pockets and \tmAXaHalfThousandModel{} and \tmAXaSingleThousandModel{} s for 1{,}000 pockets. These are \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} and \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} times the model time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} and \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times for 1{,}000 pockets. The featurization of the ligands on the CPU takes \tmAFeat{} s for every network, so that from SMILES to scores the cross-attention network needs \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenFull{} times the time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandFull{} times for 1{,}000 pockets. The ragged implementation of the cross-attention network scores \tmAPaddedSingle{} times (float32) and \tmAPaddedHalf{} times (float16) as many pairs per second as the padded implementation that we wrote first, and the ratios of this section use the ragged implementation. One more pocket costs the dual encoder \tmADualPerPocketMs{} ms and the pooled network \tmAPooledPerPocketMs{} ms of model time, and it costs the cross-attention network \tmAXaHalfPerPocket{} s in float16 and \tmAXaSinglePerPocket{} s in float32. Table~\ref{tab:cost} lists the end-to-end times.

Uni-Dock docks \dockRateFastest{} ligands per second in its fastest search mode, which puts the \pocketPairs{} million pairs of the library against 57 pockets at \pocketDockDaysFast{} GPU-days, and the dual encoder ranks the same pairs from SMILES in \tmADualFiftySevenFull{} s (Figure~\ref{fig:cost}). The times count the model of the networks and the engine of the docking. Docking adds the preparation of the ligand files, and the networks add the docking labels of their training pockets (40{,}000 ligands for each of \kpNTargets{} targets, 1.56 million dockings or \trainLabelDays{} GPU-days at the rate of the detail mode at 4{,}000 ligands per call). The ranking is the first stage of a screen: a shortlist of the best 10\% of the ranking costs a tenth of docking the library and holds the share of the top-1\% hits that the next section reports.
