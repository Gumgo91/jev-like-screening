\subsection{Discussion}

The Jev-like organization turns a screen of a library against many pockets into a matrix product. On one \tmAGpu{} the dual encoder ranks all \tmALig{} ligands of DOCKSTRING against 57 pockets from SMILES in \tmADualFiftySevenFull{} s, of which \tmAFeat{} s is the featurization of the ligands. The same ranking against 1{,}000 pockets takes \tmADualThousandFull{} s, whereas the fastest search mode of the docking engine needs \pocketDockDaysFast{} GPU-days for the 57 pockets. The ligand embedding is computed once for the library, and every further pocket costs a matrix product. One further pocket adds \tmADualPerPocketMs{} to \tmAPooledPerPocketMs{} ms to the cost of the Jev-like networks and \tmAXaHalfPerPocket{} to \tmAXaSinglePerPocket{} s to the cost of a joint network. For 1{,}000 pockets the cross-attention network needs \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times the model time of the dual encoder and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandFull{} times its time from SMILES to scores. For 57 pockets the end-to-end ratio is only \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenFull{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenFull{}, because the featurization of the ligands, which every network needs, dominates the time of the dual encoder. The organization therefore pays in the screen of a library against hundreds of pockets or more, such as the genome-wide screen of DrugCLIP over about 10{,}000 human proteins,\cite{drugclip} where all pockets share the ligand embedding.

The organization gives up a few points of the recall of the top-1\% docking hits at a budget of the best 10\% of the ranking. The dual encoder keeps \kfRatioDual{}\% and the pooled network \kfRatioPooled{}\% of the recall of the cross-attention network on the known pockets. The pocket state adds \kfDiffDualConstTen{} to \kfDiffXattnXattnConstTen{} to the recall of a network that sees only the ligand, so all three networks use the pocket of their training data. The dual encoder is the cheapest first stage, and the cross-attention network is the most accurate. The pooled network has the model time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \kfDiffPooledDualTen{} more recall when the checkpoint is chosen on known pockets. A shortlist of the best 10\% of the ranking costs a tenth of docking the library. Once the networks are trained, the shortlist saves a factor of ten in the docking of the library at the recall of Table~\ref{tab:known}. The best 20\% of the ranking hold \kfDualTwenty{} to \kfXattnTwenty{} of the hits. The docked training sample of 40{,}000 ligands per pocket is \trainShareLib{}\% of the library, so the first screen of the library, with the sample and the shortlist docked, costs \shareLabelShortlist{}\% of docking the library, a saving of a factor of \factorLabelShortlist{}. Every further library that the networks screen saves the factor of ten. The choice of the checkpoint matters for the cross-attention network. When the checkpoint is chosen on new targets, the three networks are within \kpSpreadTen{} of each other. The cross-attention network then stops after \kpXaBestLo{} to \kpXaBestHi{} updates, against \kfXaBestLo{} to \kfXaBestHi{} updates when the checkpoint is chosen on known pockets.

The networks serve the pockets of their training data. They are trained on the docking scores of the pockets of their training data, with a docked sample of 40{,}000 ligands per pocket (\kfNTargets{} pockets across the folds of this study). The screens of this study rank new ligands against these known pockets, which is the setting of a docked training sample for every pocket of interest. For a pocket outside the training data the networks have no target-specific knowledge to apply. The analyses of the preceding study in the Supporting Information show that the ranking then follows the ligand ranking that the training pockets share. A pocket that is not in the training data therefore needs docking scores of its own before this organization can screen it. The recall is measured against the Vina scores of DOCKSTRING. A ranking is not a docking score, and the shortlist still has to be docked.

DrugCLIP is Jev-like in this sense.\cite{drugclip} PharmacoNet computes the pocket model once and then aligns every ligand conformer to it by graph matching,\cite{pharmaconet} so its ligand side depends on the pocket. The speed-ups of ten million and 3{,}000 over docking that DrugCLIP and PharmacoNet report refer to other tasks and hardware. The released checkpoints, the timing code and the per-stage records allow the same cost measurement for other pocket-conditioned networks on DOCKSTRING. The alanine-truncation re-dockings of the pocket-edit check of the Supporting Information test how a network responds to an edited pocket. Future work will compare these published networks with the networks of this study on the same pockets and ligands.
