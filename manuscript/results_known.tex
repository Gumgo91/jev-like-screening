\subsection{On known pockets the Jev-like networks keep most of the recall of the joint network}

At a budget of the best 10\% of the ranking, the dual encoder recovers \kfDualTen{} of the top-1\% docking hits of the \kfNTargets{} known pockets (95\% interval \kfDualTenLo{} to \kfDualTenHi{}), the pooled network \kfPooledTen{} (\kfPooledTenLo{} to \kfPooledTenHi{}) and the cross-attention network \kfXattnTen{} (\kfXattnTenLo{} to \kfXattnTenHi{}; Table~\ref{tab:known}). A network with one constant dummy pocket for every target recovers \kfConstTen{} (\kfConstTenLo{} to \kfConstTenHi{}), the recall that the ligand alone supports. The pocket state adds \kfDiffDualConstTen{} (\kfDiffDualConstTenLo{} to \kfDiffDualConstTenHi{}) for the dual encoder, \kfDiffPooledPooledConstTen{} (\kfDiffPooledPooledConstTenLo{} to \kfDiffPooledPooledConstTenHi{}) for the pooled network and \kfDiffXattnXattnConstTen{} (\kfDiffXattnXattnConstTenLo{} to \kfDiffXattnXattnConstTenHi{}) for the cross-attention network. Every network was trained with the same recipe on the docking scores of known pockets and evaluated on the pockets of its training data against 10{,}000 ligands per pocket that were not used in training. The cross-fitted networks chose their checkpoint on seen pockets, as the setting of this study asks. Five folds give every pocket four networks per condition, and the evaluation ligands were not used in the choice of these checkpoints.

The cross-attention network is the most accurate of the three. Its recall exceeds that of the dual encoder by \kfDiffXattnDualTen{} (\kfDiffXattnDualTenLo{} to \kfDiffXattnDualTenHi{}) and that of the pooled network by \kfDiffXattnPooledTen{} (\kfDiffXattnPooledTenLo{} to \kfDiffXattnPooledTenHi{}). The dual encoder keeps \kfRatioDual{}\% (\kfRatioDualLo{} to \kfRatioDualHi{}) and the pooled network \kfRatioPooled{}\% (\kfRatioPooledLo{} to \kfRatioPooledHi{}) of the recall of the cross-attention network at the 10\% budget. The pooled network has the model time of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and recovers \kfDiffPooledDualTen{} (\kfDiffPooledDualTenLo{} to \kfDiffPooledDualTenHi{}) more of the hits than the dual encoder. At the 20\% budget the dual encoder, the pooled network and the cross-attention network recover \kfDualTwenty{}, \kfPooledTwenty{} and \kfXattnTwenty{}. A longer recipe (learning rate $10^{-4}$, up to 150{,}000 updates) raises the recall of the dual encoder to \kfDualLongTen{} and that of the cross-attention network to \kfXattnLongTen{}. The dual encoder then keeps \kfRatioDualLong{}\% (\kfRatioDualLongLo{} to \kfRatioDualLongHi{}) of the recall of the cross-attention network. The additional accuracy costs model time. The model time of the cross-attention network is \tmARatioXaHalfFiftySevenModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleFiftySevenModel{} times that of the dual encoder for 57 pockets and \tmARatioXaHalfThousandModel{} to \tmARatioXaSingleThousandModel{} times for 1{,}000 pockets (Figure~\ref{fig:cost}c).

The choice of the checkpoint matters more for the cross-attention network than for the Jev-like networks. The existing networks choose their checkpoint on new targets. With these checkpoints, the dual encoder, the pooled network and the cross-attention network recover \kpDualTen{}, \kpPooledTen{} and \kpXattnTen{} on the 39 training pockets, against \kpConstTen{} for the constant pocket (Table~\ref{tab:known}). The recall of the cross-attention network minus that of the dual encoder is \kpDiffXattnDualTen{} (interval \kpDiffXattnDualTenLo{} to \kpDiffXattnDualTenHi{}). The three networks are within \kpSpreadTen{} of each other. The cross-attention network stops after \kpXaBestLo{} to \kpXaBestHi{} updates, against \kfXaBestLo{} to \kfXaBestHi{} updates when the checkpoint is chosen on known pockets.
